2023 saw the second offering of the Arduino Hackathon post-COVID pandemic.
It also saw the first offering of the Arduino Hackathon to not be held at UQ's St Lucia campus, moving over to the River City Labs offices in Fortitude Valley.
The theme for the 2023 rendition of the event was that of 'automation', giving some direction whilst giving great freedom to see what attendees can achieve.

{% include figure.html img_path="/assets/images/2023/AH-1.jpg" alt_txt="" caption="Club banners lined up in front of the venue" %}

The weekend opened with a slow opening as contestants gradually made their way in, with some open-reign networking for people to begin forming teams.
The night then moved into opening presentations, with a welcome to all competitors, a briefing on the event and its theme, and introductions from some of the weekend's sponsors, [Holovision](https://holovision.com.au/) and [COREMATIC](https://corematic.com.au/).

{% include figure.html img_path="/assets/images/2023/AH-2.jpg" alt_txt="" caption="Sponsor representatives chatting with an attendee" %}

The Saturday ran quite similarly to the 2022 event, generally leaving things in the hands of the contestants as they got underway working on their projects.
Alongside this, workshops were run by the host societies which covered topics such as Arduino programming, electrical wiring and mechanical design.
This helped to foster the creativity of some of our less experienced participants, showing them a little more of what was capable with the components provided.
The participants had access to a host of mechanical and electrical components, 3D printers, and, of course, Arduinos to make their designs into reality.

{% include figure.html img_path="/assets/images/2023/AH-3.jpg" alt_txt="" caption="Club executives showing off the weekend's merch" %}

The final day saw teams work to finish their designs, and put together a pitch to sell their ideas.
To assist in this, the team organised a final workshop on the topic of Pitching by a guest presenter, Simeon Gover from the [UQ Public Speaking Society](https://www.facebook.com/UQPSS/).

The weekend then wrapped up with the closing ceremony, featuring a keynote address from the founder of River City Labs, *Shark Tank*'s Steve Baxter.
The ceremony then proceeded into presentations from teams in front of the judging panel, made up of Steve, and representatives from the event sponsors.

{% include figure.html img_path="/assets/images/2023/AH-4.jpg" alt_txt="" caption="Judging panel inspecting the winning creation" %}

The following prizes were on offer:
- First Overall: $500
- Runner-Up: $100 Woolworths voucher
- Best Presentation: $50 Woolworths voucher
- Most Masochistic Team: A firm hand-shake from the President of UQ MARS

First Place and Best Presentation was won by *Reversiball*, a team of three first-year students. 
They presented an idea for a smart tow-ball which innovates to prevent jack-knifing when towing a trailer.
The repository for their project can be found [here](https://github.com/ZacApelt/Hackathon-2023-reversiBALL-2)

The Runner-Up prize was given to the team behind 'Automatic Fire Suppression'.
They presented an idea for a device which seeks out and extinguishes fires using both image and infrared data.
The repository for their project can be found [here](https://github.com/Harry-Stone/hackathon2023)
